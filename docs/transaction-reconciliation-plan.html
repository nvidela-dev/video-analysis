01 · What we are trying to measure

The objective is to estimate how many sale-like interactions occurred during a business night, compare them with the transactions declared in the POS or ledger, and present the unmatched or ambiguous cases with footage for review. The primary metric is transaction count. Currency totals are a separate metric and require reliable monetary records; video alone does not reveal what a customer paid.

A person ≠ a visit ≠ an order ≠ a payment ≠ a receipt. One group may share one receipt; one customer may make two purchases; a pickup may have been paid earlier.

The achievable first release is a reconciliation assistant: it proposes service interactions, associates declared sales, and records a human decision. It must not report an unmatched visit as theft or an undeclared sale.

A fixed camera is now a stated requirement. POS access, receipt timestamps, overnight person-following scope, and operating hours remain unanswered. This document proposes defaults and explicitly marks decisions that need evidence.

02 · What exists today

Implemented and previously tested

  • Standalone Camera Ledger repository, TypeScript/Node backend and browser UI.
  • Frigate 0.16.2; a local file replay with person detection and recordings.
  • Manual transaction entry and JSON import; UTC normalization and register assignment.
  • Configurable ±30-second footage windows and clock offsets.
  • Live boxes, region overlays, track IDs, confidence, recent events and review summaries.
  • Local JSON persistence; seven core tests and two browser tests passed in the latest implementation run.

Not implemented

  • Counting transactions from activity or payment actions.
  • Precise zone transition history or durable counter visits.
  • Automatic POS reconciliation, deduplicated imports or shift reports.
  • Person re-identification across lost tracks or cameras.
  • Facial recognition, identity galleries or biometric storage.
  • Review verdicts, audit trail, user roles or production access controls.

03 · Agree on the unit before counting

UnitMeaningHow it is established
Declared transactionA completed sale in the authoritative POS/ledger.Unique receipt/sale ID and status, not each item or tender line.
Observed interactionA bounded service episode at the customer side.Video evidence and zone timeline; may be non-sale.
Transaction candidateAn interaction with enough evidence to review as a possible sale.Calibrated rules, possibly action evidence; still provisional.
Confirmed observed saleA reviewer can establish a sale-like exchange using the agreed evidence standard.Recorded verdict with footage, rationale and uncertainty.
MatchAn explicit association between candidate(s) and POS sale(s).Timestamp/register compatibility plus evidence; not simply proximity.

Define whether “transaction” means an order being taken, a payment being completed, or a receipt being finalized. Proposed default: count completed POS sale receipts; annotate the corresponding video service episode. Exclude voids and failed payments from completed sales, but keep them visible for explanation. Keep refunds in a separate category. An edited receipt or reprint must not increment the count.

If the “declared” source is only an end-of-night total, we can compare totals and investigate candidates, but cannot match individual receipts. If it is a handwritten list, its timestamps and completeness need a separate quality assessment.

05 · Configure the actual fixed camera

  1. Frame the service area: see the customer-side floor, cashier work area, terminal or cash exchange, and handoff area. Test crowded scenes, hats, backlighting and nighttime illumination. An overhead view may be good for zones but poor for face evidence.
  2. Keep recordings at useful resolution: use a lower-resolution detection stream if needed, while retaining a clear recording stream. Start at the existing 5 fps detection setting and measure misses before changing it.
  3. Draw physical zones: customer approach, service position, staff aisle, and pickup area. Payment surfaces may need hand/object analysis; person-foot zones cannot tell whether a hand touches a terminal.
  4. Calibrate time: synchronize clocks, measure offset against 10–20 known POS events across the night, distinguish consistent offset from variable finalization lag, and store uncertainty.
  5. Measure availability: record frame freshness, recording coverage and gaps. Unavailable footage means unknown, not zero activity.

Frigate determines zone presence from the bottom center of a detected bounding box. Therefore polygons must reflect where that point falls, not merely where the counter looks on screen. Use inertia/dwell settings to reduce edge jitter, then verify manually. Frigate zones documentation.

Do not filter away all non-service detections initially. They are useful context for explaining movement, pickups and staff activity. Display region names separately from a person’s role; location does not establish that someone is an employee.

06 · On-site architecture for your business

One business, one local installation. Run Frigate, the MQTT broker, Node workers, Postgres and the UI on your local machine or a dedicated on-site computer. There is no multi-tenant platform, cloud hosting requirement, subscription billing or SaaS account system.

Open the UI from your business LAN. Use individual local logins if several staff review records; this is access control for your own business, not a customer account system. Restrict camera and database ports to the local network. Remote access, if wanted later, can use a VPN; do not expose the services directly to the internet.

Internet may be needed initially for software and model downloads. Routine recording, inference, imports, matching and review must keep operating without internet. Test this explicitly. Back up metadata and selected evidence to another local disk or a business-controlled NAS, and test restoration. Retain existing POS vendor connectivity only where that POS requires it; the analysis system adds no cloud dependency.

Why add Postgres now? A few manual transactions fit in JSON. Durable lifecycle ingestion, restart recovery, deduplicated imports and review history need indexed queries, constraints and transactions. Preserve the existing JSON data with a one-time import; keep source footage in Frigate.

Enable a local MQTT broker and subscribe to frigate/events. Frigate publishes new/update/end messages with stable event IDs, frame times and zone changes. Store current-zone state separately from lifetime entered-zone history. Frigate MQTT reference.

MQTT alone is not a historical audit log. Persist each received update before processing; deduplicate redelivery and tolerate reordering. Record ingestion gaps. On restart, reconcile retained events through the HTTP API. Missing zone transitions generally cannot be recovered exactly from the existing lifetime-zone list: mark them incomplete rather than inventing times.

07 · Turn tracks into service episodes

Use a state machine per camera/track: outside → approaching → servicing → departed → finalized. Save zone transitions with source frame time and observation time. A person remaining in the aisle is not automatically a customer or a sale.

Provisional settingStarting valueWhat validates it
Minimum service-zone dwell3 secondsDoes it suppress walk-throughs while preserving quick purchases?
Brief tracking-gap tolerance5 secondsMeasured occlusion lengths; avoid merging adjacent customers.
Window padding around visit30 seconds before/afterOrder/payment timing and available recordings.
POS finalization toleranceMeasured from known matchesDo not assume the ±30-second clip window is also the matching tolerance.

These are pilot parameters, not proven production thresholds. A track ID change does not guarantee a new customer. Join short fragments only when position, time and trajectory support it; keep the original tracks and an uncertain link. Group visits may require manual grouping. A customer who stays for two orders may require two episodes inside one track; dwell alone will miss that distinction.

Assign evidence tiers: presence only, service interaction, visible exchange, and review-confirmed sale candidate. Unknown and non-sale outcomes must remain first-class states. Confidence thresholds are calibrated operating rules, not probabilities of payment.

08 · Detecting a payment or handoff

Start with zone/visit rules and manual review. If too many visits remain ambiguous, add a targeted action model to short candidate clips—not to every frame of the entire night.

  • Observable evidence: customer approaches terminal, cash passes across the counter, cashier opens drawer, goods are handed over, customer leaves with an item.
  • Limits: a card gesture may be a failed payment; an open drawer may be making change; a handoff may be a prepaid pickup. Items held by a person do not reveal price.
  • Candidate implementations: temporal action model, pose/hand/object cues, or a locally hosted video-capable model returning structured evidence with timestamps and an explicit unknown result.
  • Validation: assess performance on this shop’s annotated footage. Do not assume a generic model understands the terminal or local service procedure.

Store the model version, clip interval, evidence timestamps and reviewer correction. Never make a model-generated description the sole basis for an accusation or accounting adjustment. All action-model processing must run locally; benchmark model quality and hardware load before adding it.

09 · Normalize declared records

Minimum record: source + sale_id + register_id + finalized_at + timezone + status. Helpful fields: opened_at, amount in integer minor units, currency, payment type, refund/void link, order/channel and optional cashier ID from the POS. A POS cashier ID is declared system data; it is not a visual identity inference.

  • Keep raw import rows and batch checksum for traceability.
  • Unique key on source/sale_id; repeat imports update versioned records or no-op, not double count.
  • Distinguish one receipt with two tenders from two separate sale receipts.
  • Normalize times to UTC; keep original timestamps and the source timezone.
  • Store corrections, late receipts, voids, refunds and reopened orders without deleting evidence.
  • Reject missing/invalid mandatory fields into an import-error queue; report completeness.

If only a declared total is available, record its business interval and definition. Report the totals as a separate comparison; individual matching remains unavailable until receipt-level data exists.

10 · Match carefully, without reusing a receipt

  1. Restrict candidates to compatible register/camera, business interval and usable coverage.
  2. Correct camera offset and account for measured POS finalization lag. Build a candidate set using uncertainty bounds.
  3. Rank by temporal agreement, relevant service evidence and optional order/payment information. Show contributing signals; do not call a heuristic score a probability.
  4. Solve competing assignments together rather than selecting the nearest receipt independently for every visit. Standard auto-matches consume a receipt at most once.
  5. Permit explicit reviewed split/merge associations: one group → one receipt; one visit → two receipts; several visits → one tab. Preserve associations and reasons.
  6. Leave ties and weak evidence unresolved. Recompute suggestions after late imports without overwriting accepted decisions.
Example: three candidate interactions and two receipts do not automatically mean one hidden sale. The third interaction might be a pickup, a question, a tracking duplicate, or a sale whose receipt is missing. The footage and declaration completeness decide which interpretation is supportable.

Statuses: matched, ambiguous, observed candidate without declaration, declared sale without visible interaction, explained non-sale, missing footage, incomplete POS import, and pending review. “No match” is a workflow state, not a conclusion.

11 · What the night report should show

Declared

Completed receipts, separate refunds/voids, source completeness and imported interval.

Observed

Candidate interactions, reviewer-confirmed sale candidates, dismissed non-sales and unresolved cases.

Reconciliation

Accepted links, unmatched cases, uncertainty, coverage gaps and evidence availability.

Publish a reviewer-audited difference only for aligned units and covered periods: confirmed observed sale episodes − completed declared sale receipts. If split/group cases change the unit, resolve them before subtracting. A raw candidate count should be displayed but not labeled an actual transaction total.

For example, “112 candidates, 95 declared receipts, 90 accepted matches, 12 explained non-sales and 10 unresolved” is incomplete until associations and counting units are reconciled; the dashboard must not silently subtract 112 − 95 and declare 17 missing sales. Prefer a ledger of individual outcomes and a reconciled total whose components balance.

Include coverage minutes, unmatched receipts, missing-import periods, review completion, late adjustments and report version. Monetary variance is shown only when corresponding amounts are known from authoritative records or independently confirmed evidence.

13 · Following one person over the night

This is a separate feature from transaction reconciliation. Clarify whether the target is an employee, a customer, or a person selected manually in footage, and whether “follow” includes leaving and returning or changing cameras.

MethodUseful forFailure boundaries
Continuous object trackingOne person remains visible in one fixed camera.Occlusion, crowds, cuts and exit/re-entry can create new IDs. Current Frigate IDs are not all-night identities.
Manual track stitchingReviewer links visible fragments for a selected anonymous person.Requires human time; keep gaps and uncertain links visible. Best first experiment.
Appearance re-identificationSuggesting links across short gaps using clothing/body features.Similar uniforms, changed clothing, lighting and viewpoints can cause swaps. It is still person-linking technology with privacy implications.
Face matchingRecognizing an enrolled person when a usable face appears.Back-of-head views, masks, blur, IR and occlusion. A match provides an observation, not continuous knowledge of location.

Recommended first pilot: select a person in a short labeled clip, build a timeline of track segments with human-confirmed links, and show “visible,” “possible continuation,” “lost,” and “reappeared.” Never fill a visibility gap with an assumed path. Expand to a full shift only after measuring identity switches and broken tracks.

Evaluate association precision, missed reappearances, false joins, identity switches and percentage of visible time covered. Report gaps explicitly. Do not force each fragment to belong to a known person. Facial recognition is not necessary to count service interactions.

14 · Local person tracking and facial recognition

This is an installation exclusively for your business, not a SaaS product. Camera video, transaction records, detection events and any future identity data stay on equipment you control. No cloud inference service, paid image API, hosted database or per-camera subscription is part of the proposed architecture.

Facial recognition can run locally, so there is no metered charge per face or frame. Its costs are compatible hardware, electricity, setup, reference-image preparation, maintenance and manual correction. Existing equipment may be sufficient; benchmark before purchasing a dedicated machine.

Frigate documents a small CPU-oriented recognition model and a larger model intended for accelerated hardware. Its documented AVX/AVX2 requirements mean compatibility on this ARM64 Mac must be verified for the specific version. The existing person detector working does not prove face recognition will work. A supported dedicated local machine is an option if this computer cannot run the required model reliably. Frigate face recognition documentation.

Start with anonymous tracking and human-confirmed links. Face recognition remains an optional, separate local experiment for selected authorized subjects, with reference data stored locally, restricted access and an explicit deletion policy. It is not required for transaction reconciliation and cannot fill unseen periods with certainty.

16 · How we decide whether it works

  1. Build ground truth: manually label a calibration set and separate held-out test footage from the same fixed setup. Record service episodes, actual sale evidence, grouped/split orders, pickups, occlusions and POS links.
  2. Measure candidate detection: precision = real sale-like episodes among proposed candidates; recall = labeled sale-like episodes found. Record split/merge errors and absolute nightly count error.
  3. Measure matching: accepted automatic-link precision, unmatched declared receipts, missed correct links, duplicate receipt assignment and ambiguity rate.
  4. Measure operating cost: review minutes per shift, fraction needing manual review, CPU/GPU load, recording gaps and delayed imports.
  5. Test recovery: broker/app restart, out-of-order messages, duplicate imports, camera loss, midnight boundaries, late receipts, voids/refunds and missing clips.

Proposed acceptance targets, not achieved results: ≥95% recall for labeled service/sale candidates; ≥98% precision for auto-accepted standard receipt links; ≤5% absolute count error on reviewed held-out shifts. Agree these targets before evaluation and publish sample size, confidence intervals and excluded cases. A higher auto-match precision can be obtained by leaving more cases unresolved.

The edited YouTube sample proves playback and UI integration only. It cannot establish transaction-count accuracy because it has no authentic synchronized receipt ledger and changes views.
